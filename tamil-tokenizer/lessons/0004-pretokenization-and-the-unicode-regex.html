Lesson 0004

Pretokenization and the Unicode Regex

Why BPE needs boundary walls, and how pretokenizer.rs uses Unicode categories to keep Tamil words intact.

Step 4 of 13. The pipeline. Why merges must not cross word boundaries.

1. The Problem: What Happens Without Boundary Walls?

BPE is a greedy algorithm. It counts adjacent character pairs anywhere in the text. If you ran raw BPE directly on full sentences without pretokenization, three major problems would happen:

1
Words Merge Across Spaces

Common word pairs would get glued together: "வணக்கம் உலகம்" would merge into a single gigantic token. The vocabulary would quickly fill with pairs of words instead of reusable words.

2
Punctuation Glues to Words

"தமிழ்", "தமிழ்,", "தமிழ்!", and "தமிழ்?" would become four completely distinct token IDs. A word followed by a comma would not share embeddings with the same word followed by a period.

3
Special Control Tokens Get Shredded

Control tokens like <|startoftext|> or <|endoftext|> would get cut into regular characters (<, |, start), breaking the language model's control signals.

2. The Solution: Pretokenization Sets Boundary Walls

Before BPE merges anything, pretokenizer.rs slices the text into isolated chunks.

The Golden Rule of Pretokenization

BPE merges can never cross chunk boundaries. Each chunk is tokenized strictly on its own.

For example, the sentence "வணக்கம், உலகம்! 123" is split into:

["வணக்கம்", ",", " உலகம்", "!", " 123"]

Because "," and "!" are their own chunks, punctuation can never merge into Tamil words.

3. Inside pretokenizer.rs: The Unicode Regex

Open pretokenizer.rs:19-31 to inspect the 11 regex patterns:

A
Special Tokens First: <\|[a-z_]+\|>

Matches tokens like <|startoftext|> first, preventing any later rule from touching them.

B
The Tamil Word Rule: ?(?:[\p{Lt}\p{Lm}\p{Lo}]\p{M}*)+

This is the core rule for Tamil text. Three key pieces:

  • ?: Matches an optional leading space, binding the space to the start of the word.
  • \p{Lo}: Unicode category "Letter, other". Tamil letters have no upper/lower case, so they belong to \p{Lo}.
  • \p{M}*: Zero or more combining marks! This keeps the pulli (்) and vowel signs (ா, ி, ீ...) glued to their consonant. Without \p{M}*, கா would be sliced into க and a detached ா.
C
Numbers: (?:\p{N}\p{M}*)+

Groups sequences of digits into independent number chunks.

D
Punctuation: ?(?:[\p{P}\p{S}]\p{M}*)+

Isolates commas, periods, quotes, and mathematical symbols.
